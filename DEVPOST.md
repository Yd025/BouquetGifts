# Bouquet ✿ — gifts grown by everyone who loves them

## Inspiration
Every friend group has one person who ends up organizing the gift. They guess what to buy, put it on their own card, and then spend three weeks chasing Venmo requests. The rest of the group knows things that would have made the gift better ("she's been shooting film all summer"), but none of it gets to the person buying it. Group-gift tools only collect money. We wanted to change the whole shopping journey for a group buying together: how they discover the gift, decide on it, pay for it, and give it.

## AI between people and payments (Visa: Reimagine Shopping)
Visa asked us to show how AI can **bridge the gap between humans and the payment experience**. Group payments break down at exactly the moments where people talk like people and payment systems want numbers. Bouquet puts Gemini in those gaps, and keeps the money maths deterministic:
- **Chip in by just saying it, conditions included.** Tap 🎙 and say *"thirty now, forty-two if we get the record"* (the browser's free Web Speech API, or type it). Gemini reads it into a hold and a condition, and the read-back is built to be read in three seconds: a three-column **Now $30 · Max $42 · More on Fri**, then one big line, **"$42 cap, only for the Record tier"**, and one sentence: *"Hold $30 now. +$12 only if the group goes for the Record."* That extra $12 is offered as a one-tap top-up only when the group is going for that tier, or held right away if it finishes the tier with the group's holds. If a phrase fits two tiers ("the record" when the table has a record player *and* a record), Bouquet asks **"Which one?"** with one tap per tier instead of guessing. Paydays work the same way (*"$30, $40 max, I get paid Friday"*), and if payday falls after the buy-by date no top-up is scheduled and the friend is warned. Two number fields can't do that. Gemini can't invent money: an amount the person never said is thrown out, and the hold is capped by their own budget.
- **A fair, private top-up plan.** When the group is short of the next tier, Bouquet splits the gap in whole dollars **in proportion to each friend's remaining room** (cap minus what they already hold), so nobody is asked for more than they said they could do and the ask that falls on each friend is fair. It asks friends who can pay now first, adds payday friends only if that isn't enough, and never asks anyone twice. Each friend sees only their own ask plus a count, never an amount for anyone else: *"💐 Top up $12? Maya's gift unlocks the Record · **+$12 from you · 3 other friends are getting their own ask**."* One tap on their saved card (a mock-Visa token; we never store the card number) runs the **carrier SIM-swap check first** and shows *"📶 phone check passed"*. Rules size every ask; Gemini only words it.
- **"Explain my payment" as a receipt.** At any point, each friend sees what happens to *their* money as three lines: **Charged $29.70 · Released $0.30 · If checked out now: tier 2 of 3 (Kodak Gold film), $98 in total, is fully funded**, with the full reasoning behind a "Why?" toggle: *"If Olive checks out now, you pay $29.70 of your $30 hold and $0.30 goes back to your card. Why: tier 2 (Kodak Gold film) costs $98 in total and the group holds $99, so it is fully funded. Visa would capture exactly $98, split in proportion to each hold, and release the other $1."* The rules compute every figure from the ledger. Gemini only rewrites the wording, and its version is shown only if **every dollar amount matches exactly and none is added**. Tests check that "fully funded" means held ≥ price and that everyone's shares add up to exactly the captured amount.
- **Gift concierge.** While the group is picking, the concierge ranks the gifts on the table by the trait cards people *kept*, the votes and everyone's budget caps added together. It drops near-duplicates. Each pick gets one line on **what sets it apart** (*"The only pick that draws on all 3 friends' cards"*, *"The only one your budget covers in full today ($90)"*) and names every friend whose card it uses, and whose it doesn't. Budget is phrased as what's reachable for that gift (*"Caps could reach $98 of $237 (2 of 3 items) if everyone goes to their cap"*, or *"Reachable if everyone goes up to their cap"*), never as a total of everyone's caps, so no one can work out a friend's private number.
- **Honest about the sandbox:** Visa isn't issuing API credentials this weekend, so holds, captures and releases run on our **mock Visa sandbox**. It behaves like a real authorization flow (Luhn check, a declined test card, idempotent holds, capture ≤ hold, release). Every AI feature also has a deterministic fallback, and the UI labels which one answered ("read by Gemini" vs "read by Bouquet rules").

## What it does
Bouquet is a group gift played as a card game.
- **Plant:** the organizer sets the recipient, the occasion, and when the gift has to arrive. Bouquet subtracts shipping time to get a **buy-by deadline**.
- **Invite:** when the gift is already funding, the invite link opens **one money-first screen** on the phone: the gift photo, *"$99 in from 3 friends"*, quick amounts **$15 / $25 / $40** (or just say it), a name and number, and **"Hold $25 with Visa"**, with one line underneath on the most you'd pay, the deadline, who orders and ships it, and that the hold drops off if the group doesn't check out. No account and no signup wall: guests hold as guests. The card questionnaire comes after, as an optional *"Add something for Maya (30 seconds)"* with a Skip. Earlier in the game, friends tap a face-down card and join with a name and number, no OTP or app. Before any hold of $50+ and before checkout, Bouquet asks the network whether that SIM was **swapped recently** (CAMARA SIM Swap via Nokia Network as Code), the classic account-takeover signal.
- **Card:** one question per screen. Shared questions come first, then random ones **drawn from a deck**, so every card is different. The art grows as you answer, and minting tears open a pack. Each card's title comes from the friend's own answers, with stat chips like *Sunrise Hiker ×2*, and no two friends in a bouquet get the same title. The chips follow the title: most answers first, and on a tie the trait the title names comes first, so *The Caffeinated Sunrise Hiker* lists *Coffee Snob ×1* before *Bookworm ×1*. Every card has its own flower back and procedural SVG art. Gemini image generation is wired in and swaps in when the key has image quota. One answer ("which gift would annoy them?") filters out bad gifts.
- **Draw & fuse:** everyone's answers become a **deck of trait cards** with common, rare and legendary frames that shimmer. Each friend draws from the deck and **keeps (swipe right) or discards (swipe left)** each card, Tinder-style, with buttons and arrow keys as an alternative and an Undo. Kept cards fan out into a **hand**. Select or drag two hand cards together and they **fuse** into a gift card, so *Sunrise Hiker + Coffee Snob* becomes a summit pour-over kit. **Rare fusions only unlock when 2+ friends agree**, and every gift cites whose answers produced it. A fusion counts as your vote, and the gift table shows live tallies. Every gift on the table, including the safe-pick Treasure Box and wildcard Duo Boxes, is made of **real products for its traits** and shows a real product photo, with no photo repeated on one table, even after a friend fuses a new gift from their hand.
- **Bloom:** each friend sets **their own budget** (or just says it in plain words, see above) and places a **Visa hold** (mock sandbox), not a charge. The gift gets bigger in tiers as people join. Each tier is a **real product with its real photo, store link and price** (a GSI pour-over set, then a TIMEMORE grinder, then Fellow beans), and the thresholds are the actual cumulative prices. The bouquet on screen grows as it fills, and the gift itself is shown as a strip of its products that light up one by one as tiers are funded. A **Who's in** list shows who is holding, next to the buy-by countdown. The API sends each friend only a *holding* flag for everyone else, never their amount or cap. With exactly two holders, the room total would let one friend subtract their own hold to get the other's, so it shows the highest funded tier instead of the exact figure. Holding already? The button says "Add $20 more with Visa", so nobody wonders whether it replaces their hold.
- **Checkout:** before anyone checks out, each friend sees their exact share ("If the group checks out now, you pay $22 of your $45 hold"), and an over-funded gift says where the extra goes ("Checkout charges exactly $96 … the extra $39 is released, never charged"). The preview runs the same allocation code as checkout. After checkout, in plain English ("You were charged $38.18, not $45, because the group funded tier 2"), holds are captured at the **highest tier the group fully funded**, split fairly in proportion to each hold. Anything left over is released. If the deadline passes, **every hold is released** and nobody pays. Once bought, the room header stops counting down and says **"✓ Purchased in time"**.
- **Reveal:** the recipient tears open a pack. Each card turns a trait into a compliment ("You chase every sunrise") and a **Gemini TTS narrator** reads that friend's own memory aloud. The finale is a holo legendary gift card in a **garland** with confetti.
- **Works on a phone:** judges and friends usually open the invite link on a phone. At 390px nothing scrolls sideways, inputs are 16px (so iOS doesn't zoom), buttons are at least 44px tall, the minted card shrinks so **"Add to the bouquet"** stays on screen, the landing pitch fits with nothing cut off, the nav stays on one screen width, the gift table stacks into one column, and the friend cards become a swipeable row instead of a stack of full-screen cards.
- **Judge demo drawer** (a small 🎬 button that sits in the page margin, clear of the panels): try a declined card, simulate a SIM-swapped friend (the carrier check blocks the hold), fast-forward past the deadline (every hold is released), and **Reset demo** (releases holds and reseeds the Maya bouquet, so every judge starts from the same state). The dev clock and sweep routes require a signed-in member of that bouquet, and reset only works for the demo account.

## How we built it
- **Next.js 14** frontend with hand-drawn SVG: procedural flower card backs, a bouquet that blooms with funding, and the garland frame. Cards use 3D flip animations.
- **Express + TypeScript** API on **Postgres**, with an embedded PGlite (WASM Postgres) for tests. The app is a state machine: collecting → fusing → funding → purchased → revealed, or expired.
- **Money safety:** amounts are integer cents, ledger events are append-only, and the funded total is always the sum of the ledger, never a stored counter. Holds are idempotent, and checkout uses `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`. A hold re-checks the friend's private cap inside the transaction (`FOR UPDATE` on their member row), so two simultaneous holds can't go past it; a concurrency test fires them in parallel. We also tested double-click checkouts and checkout racing the deadline sweeper.
- **Adapters:** a payments service (mock Visa holds, capture and release) built to swap in a real processor, **T-Mobile CAMARA via Nokia Network as Code** (`api/src/nac.ts`: a real RapidAPI client for the SIM Swap check, `X-RapidAPI-Key`/`X-RapidAPI-Host` headers, Nokia's simulator numbers, with the mock as a silent fallback on any error; Number Verify stays mocked), and **Google Gemini** for card titles, memory polish and gift pitches (`gemini-2.5-flash`), card art (`gemini-2.5-flash-image`, wired; falls back to SVG when there's no quota) and the reveal narrator (`gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts`), with every generated image and voice line cached so it is made once. Every adapter has a deterministic mock so the demo works offline.
- **Live room on Cloudflare:** a Worker plus a **Durable Object per bouquet** (WebSocket Hibernation) fans out votes, funding and the reveal to everyone in the room, with presence. The API signs short-lived member tickets with HMAC. If the socket drops, the room reconnects with backoff; if the worker is unreachable, it falls back to SSE and then polling.
- **Real catalog:** 37 products from 16 US stores, covering every recipe and every trait. Product data and photos come from each store's public product JSON, and the script refuses any store whose `/meta.json` isn't USD. Photos are cached locally and served from R2 or local storage, and tier thresholds are the real cumulative prices.
- **AI bridge guardrails:** chip-in uses Gemini JSON mode (`responseSchema`). Amounts must appear in what the person typed, dates must be real and near-future, and holds are clamped to the cap. Explanations and concierge lines pass a figure check (same `$` amounts in, same out) before display. Each feature works fully without Gemini.
- **Testing:** Vitest unit tests (consensus, tiers, pro-rata capture fuzzed across 200 cases, the chip-in parser, the figure check), API integration tests (including Gemini answers that invent amounts and are rejected), and Playwright end-to-end runs. One plays an organizer, three friends and the recipient. Another drives the concierge, a plain-words chip-in through to a Visa hold, and the explainer before and after checkout.

## Challenges
- Splitting a capture fairly in integer cents, so it never exceeds any single hold and always adds up exactly (we used a largest-remainder split).
- Making "rare fusion" mean something. It requires distinct friends to agree, not the same friend picking a trait twice.
- Handling a buy-by deadline that can fire during checkout.

## Accomplishments
A working flow from the first invite to the garland reveal, in which no single friend ever fronts the money and the gift is picked by what the whole group knows about the person.

## What we learned
Payments logic is more about idempotency, locking, and the ledger than about calling a charge API.

## Business model / revenue (DubHacks NEXT)
- **Who pays:** the group, the merchants, and companies.
- **Take rate on group gifts.** A small fee (we plan 4%) on the captured amount, shown up front: the checkout preview already shows it ("Bouquet fee: $0 in this demo. Planned: 4% of what's actually captured"). It replaces the hidden cost of one friend fronting $150 and chasing Venmo for three weeks. A $120 gift earns about $4–6. The fee only applies when a gift is actually bought, so a gift that expires costs nobody anything.
- **Merchant affiliate and placement fees.** Every tier is a real product from a real store, so Bouquet works as a high-intent referral channel: a group that has already agreed and already funded is a near-certain conversion. Affiliate commission is typically 5–10%. Later, stores can pay to be a recipe's featured product, labelled as sponsored.
- **Premium occasions for companies and teams.** Office birthdays, farewells and baby showers for a team: a manager-funded base tier, colleagues top up, expense-ready receipts, Slack invites and a company catalog. Priced per seat or per occasion (around $3–5 per employee per month). This is the recurring-revenue segment.
- **Why they'd pay:** the group gets a better gift with no awkward money chasing. Merchants get pre-funded, consensus-picked orders. Companies get a gifting ritual that runs itself.
- **First users:** college friend groups (birthdays and graduations, which is us), then campus clubs and student orgs, then small teams at startups.

## How it compares
- **Money pools and payment apps** (Venmo or Cash App requests, group collection pages): one person still picks the gift and fronts or chases the money, and contributions are charged up front. Bouquet places *holds*, captures only the tier the group actually funded, and releases everything if the deadline passes.
- **Group cards and collection sites** (GroupTogether, Kudoboard and similar): great for messages plus a pot of money, but the pot isn't tied to a specific gift, and nobody's knowledge shapes what gets bought.
- **Wishlists and gift exchanges** (Elfster, Giftster): they coordinate who buys what, but each person still pays alone.
- **What's different:** the gift comes out of what friends know (cards → fusion → vote), each friend keeps a private cap and only sees their own fair top-up ask, payment wording comes from Gemini but every figure comes from the ledger, and the gift scales in real-product tiers with the money actually raised. (This is our read of these products' public pages, not a feature-by-feature audit.)

## Tracks we're entering
- **Visa: Reimagine Shopping.** AI bridging people and payments: a plain-words, payday-aware chip-in, a private one-tap top-up plan, a per-person payment explainer and the gift concierge, on a mock Visa sandbox (no credentials are issued this weekend). Holds, fair-share capture, release on deadline.
- **T-Mobile.** CAMARA SIM Swap before large holds and before capture, through Nokia Network as Code. What's real: the SIM Swap client is the documented NaC call (`POST /passthrough/camara/v1/sim-swap/sim-swap/v0/check`, `{phoneNumber, maxAge}` → `{swapped}`), used whenever `NAC_RAPIDAPI_KEY` is set, with Nokia's simulator numbers in the demo (+99999991000 = swapped, +99999991001 = clean; seeded Jon uses the clean one). What's mocked: **Number Verify** (it needs a device-side OAuth flow over mobile data, which a web join form can't do), and any SIM Swap call that fails falls back to the mock silently. Honest status: Nokia's documented gateway (`network-as-code.p-eu.rapidapi.com`) refused the TLS handshake from every network we tried this weekend, so `check:keys` reports `Nokia NaC (SIM Swap) FAIL` and the demo answers from the mock. The gateway is configurable (`NAC_BASE_URL`, `NAC_RAPIDAPI_HOST`), and `/api/health` shows whether the last live call succeeded.
- **Cloudflare (free tier only).** Turnstile on join, holds and the reveal; the live room is a Worker plus a SQLite-backed Durable Object per bouquet (Workers Free plan). R2 is optional: we fall back to local storage, so we don't depend on anything paid.
- **MLH Best Use of Gemini API.** `gemini-2.5-flash` for card titles, memory polish, gift pitches, chip-in understanding (JSON mode), payment explanations, top-up nudges and the concierge (falling back to `gemini-flash-lite-latest` when the free daily limit runs out); `gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts` narrates the reveal. Image generation is wired but our key has no image quota, so cards use procedural SVG art.
